Instrument audit — K6, colour channel predictability (revision 6)

Scope: the column measures second-order predictability of a channel from the position and from
  the channels selected before it — not full statistical independence.
Script: tools/experiments/k6_colour_predictability.py; unit tests:
  tests/unit/test_k6_colour_predictability.py. Result: data/refapp/K6_result.json, committed —
  the FIT ranking (axis ids by rank), the selected channels and every reported figure.
Data: definitions.md (2,221 vectors, 8-axis basis). The script refuses to run unless
  sha256(embeddings.npy) = eafb0e97172830f2404e96fa08d74bf6cccc0b6cbe84d47b790a476603e7d8d1 and
  sha256(labels.json) = 1d60699353d810f089730c6203ee28f9c416e3004b60781bc965cec284097f4f (the
  digests in the artifact's PROVENANCE.json), which fixes the row order. Split once by position:
  FIT = even indices (1,111 vectors), EVAL = odd indices (1,110). The frame is fitted on FIT
  only; every R², null and control is computed on EVAL only. X_c = the 1,110 × 384 EVAL matrix
  centred on its column means.
Frame (one per epoch): rank â_1…â_8 by the mean over FIT of ⟨v, â_k⟩, descending; ties broken
  by axis id, ascending. The ranking from the full 2,221 vectors is also computed and reported:
  whether it matches the FIT ranking, descriptive only. Anchor ĉ₁ = rank 1. Poles are always
  the normalised â. Each dipole's c_A is the higher-ranked and c_B the lower-ranked axis of
  its pair: dipole 1 = ranks (2, 3), dipole 2 = (4, 5), dipole 3 = (6, 7); rank 8 left over.
Collinear fallback (‖P⊥c_A − P⊥c_B‖ < 1e-6, eps_collinear default at polar_projector.py:58),
  logged per dipole:
  dipole 1 on the fallback → the instrument is invalid and the run stops (x and the positive
  control are undefined as a contrast); dipole 2 or 3 on the fallback → that dipole's λ is not
  a candidate.
Position: x = λ₁, y = ⟨v, ĉ₁⟩.
Candidates, in preference order:
  λ₂; λ₃; ⟨v, P⊥â_(8)⟩ / ‖P⊥â_(8)‖², â_(8) the rank-8 axis (not axis id 8), projected like every
  other channel, orthogonal to ĉ₁;
  l = 1/(1 + Var) of the 8 projections ⟨v, â_k⟩, population variance (ddof = 0). l shares
  inputs with the position by construction: its terms include ⟨v, ĉ₁⟩ = y and the two dipole-1
  axes that define x. It is kept, last in order, and the rule decides.
Clipping: every λ-type channel (x, λ₂, λ₃, the null) enters the regression unclipped,
  ⟨v, w⟩ / ‖w‖²; clipping is a rendering step, not information. The fraction of vectors that
  would clip is reported per arm, descriptive only.
Null (uniform in P⊥, deciding): 1,000 channels ⟨v, w_i⟩ / ‖w_i‖² with w_i = P⊥g_i / ‖P⊥g_i‖,
  g_i = rng.standard_normal(384): directions uniform on the unit sphere of ĉ₁⊥.
  rng = numpy.random.Generator(numpy.random.PCG64(20260918)), the generator
  numpy.random.default_rng(20260918) returns; g_1…g_1000 drawn in that order, before
  anything else. Why this reference (an explicit choice, fixed before the run): every candidate
  direction is fixed by the basis — P⊥(â_A − â_B), P⊥â_(8) — and by a ranking fitted on FIT, never
  on the EVAL vectors it is judged on; the null directions are likewise chosen without EVAL,
  under the one constraint all candidates share (orthogonal to ĉ₁). Only a direction's in-span
  part affects R².
Null (Σ-weighted, reference only): 1,000 channels as above with g_i = X_cᵀ z_i / √1110,
  z_i = rng.standard_normal(1110), drawn after the deciding null from the same generator.
  Weights directions toward the leading principal components; reported per step as the 989th
  smallest of its 1,000 R² values on B_s (tau_sigma_reference), never used by the rule.
Column "R²_s" (selection step s = 1, 2, 3): ordinary least squares of the channel on
  B_s = (1, x̃, ỹ, x̃², ỹ², x̃ỹ, c̃_1, …, c̃_{s−1}), with c_j the channels already selected and
  x̃ = z(x), ỹ = z(y), c̃_j = z(c_j) (z(·) below), the products formed after standardising. Why:
  x, y and each c_j are independent dimensions, and a dimension's scale carries no information
  about it — a narrow range is not a smaller dimension. Standardising maps span(B_s) onto the
  span of the raw columns (1, x, y, x², y², xy, c_1, …), so every R² is unchanged (D6); it only
  removes conditioning that comes from units. Centred R² = 1 − SS_res / SS_tot, SS_tot about the
  channel's mean; in-sample on EVAL, n = 1,110. Adjusted R² = 1 − (1 − R²)(n − 1)/(n − k), k =
  number of columns of B_s including the constant, reported alongside. Every candidate still
  remaining at step s is logged with its outcome (selected, discarded, not_reached); R² is
  computed only for the candidates tested at that step.
Threshold τ_s: np.sort(R²)[988] — the 989th smallest of the deciding null's 1,000 R² values on
  the same basis B_s, recomputed at every step. Derivation: a candidate that behaves like a null
  direction is exchangeable with the 1,000 null values, so it exceeds the 989th with probability
  12/1001 = 1.20%; each candidate is tested at most once (a fail discards it, a pass selects
  it), so across four candidates (Bonferroni) the chance of excluding at least one truly
  unpredictable candidate by accident is ≤ 4 × 12/1001 = 4.80% ≤ 5%. The exchangeability
  argument holds for the three projection candidates only; l is not a projection onto a
  direction, so for l the null is a declared reference, not a derived one. If dipole 2 or 3 is
  on the fallback, fewer than four candidates remain and the threshold is unchanged —
  conservative, declared.
Rule (sequential): at step s, the first remaining candidate in preference order with
  R²_s ≤ τ_s is selected as c_s; a candidate that fails at step s is discarded. A step with no
  remaining candidate does not run (no selection, no calibration controls) and is logged.
  Fewer than three selected → the missing channels are constant.
Positive control: h = tanh(d_esc) of dipole 1, as the operator computes it (λ₁ clipped).
  Must satisfy R²_1(h) > τ_1. Limit: when λ₁ is not clipped, d_esc² = 1 − y² − ‖v_dipole,1‖²·x²,
  so h tests detection of an exact, nonlinear redundancy only.
Calibration controls, at every step s (exact R² by construction): ĥ_s = the OLS fit of z(h) on
  B_s; n⊥_s = the first deciding-null channel, residualised on B_s; z(·) = centre on the EVAL
  mean and divide by the population standard deviation (ddof = 0). For target ρ,
  m_ρ = a·z(ĥ_s) + b·z(n⊥_s) with a² / (a² + b²) = ρ; since n⊥_s ⊥ B_s in-sample,
  R²_s(m_ρ) = ρ exactly. Two controls per step: ρ_in = τ_s / 2 (must be admissible) and
  ρ_out = (1 + τ_s) / 2 (must not be); each measured R²_s must equal its ρ within 1e-9.
  Tolerance: float64 least squares on at most 9 columns has forward error ≈ cond(B_s)·ε,
  ε = 2.2e-16; the run requires cond(B_s) ≤ 1e5 (bound ≈ 2e-11, fifty times below 1e-9) and
  reports cond(B_s) and every measured deviation. cond(B_s) is that of the standardised basis
  above, the matrix the regression is computed on. cond(B_s) > 1e5 → the instrument is invalid
  for conditioning, reported as such, distinct from a defect in the regression.
  What they detect: a wrong regression, a wrongly augmented basis B_s, a reversed comparison.
  What they cannot detect: a wrong τ_s — they are placed relative to whatever τ_s is computed.
Unit tests (committed with the script, reviewed in phase 2; they can fail): the threshold
  function returns 989 on the input 1…1000; FIT and EVAL are the even and odd indices and
  disjoint; B_s has exactly the columns listed, in order, standardised, at each step; the first
  component of g_1, g_2, g_3 from seed 20260918 matches values recorded in the test (this pins
  the numpy stream; the directions themselves depend on ĉ₁, i.e. on the data); candidate,
  null and control channels go through one regression function.

Same thing in every arm?   Every candidate, control and null channel on the same vectors, frame,
                           unclipped form, basis B_s and regression. Declared difference: l is not
                           a projection and shares inputs with the position.
Leakage?                   The frame is fitted on FIT and judged on EVAL; no label or ground
                           truth is read. FIT and EVAL are disjoint but not independent: the
                           even/odd split puts adjacent chunks of the same text in both halves.
                           The comparison with the full-corpus ranking is the real check.
Comparable arms?           Valid only if dipole 1 is not on the fallback, R²_1(h) > τ_1, every
                           calibration control lands on its stated side of τ_s at its exact ρ,
                           and the unit tests pass. If any fails, the instrument is invalid and
                           no candidate result is used: the result carries valid = false, the
                           first failed condition and the full list, and a consumer (R4) must
                           refuse it. Only dipole 1 on the fallback stops the run; conditions
                           are checked in this order: fallback; then per step cond(B_s), the
                           positive control (step 1), each control's exactness and its side.
                           Geodetic axes are not random: if they
                           come out more predictable than the null, that is a result for the
                           author, not a reason to relax the rule.
Text matches code?         Checked at review (phase 2).
