# R5 — map vs. list (exploratory)

**Question.** Is navigating the map less useful than a plain list of related notes? (`POC.md`, R5.)
Exploratory: one user, who wrote the tasks and judges them; labelled exploration, not result. It has
no row in Claims.

## Views

- **Map:** the perspective at X (select X and the map is redrawn from it). Zoom, pan, selecting
  nodes and pointing at a node, which shows its id and text, are allowed; the related-notes list and
  the note panel's relations toggle are not used.
- **List:** the 15 nearest notes to X (feature 5). The map and the relations toggle are not used.

## Tasks

Every task is run in both views. Odd tasks start on the map and even tasks on the list, so neither
view is always first; the second run is on a later calendar day than the first. Written before day 5
and frozen by the commit that adds them. "Expected" holds propositions of the *Ethics* as

### Where the expected propositions come from

The author has not read the *Ethics*, so no expected set comes from the author's memory of it, and
none comes from what a view shows: that would measure the engine against itself.

- **T01–T05.** Question: "find the notes this sentence rests on". One anchor per Part, in Part
  order. A candidate is a sentence of a demonstration, corollary or scholium that cites at least 3
  propositions other than its own (`Prop. n`, `(II. xl. note)`, `(I. 36)`; definitions, axioms,
  postulates and lemmas are ignored). The anchor is the candidate in the middle position of its Part
  in reading order (1 of 1, 4 of 8, 6 of 12, 7 of 14, 5 of 10; 45 candidates), chosen by that rule
  before any view was opened. Expected = the propositions the sentence cites, checked by hand against
  each of the five sentences.
- **T06–T10.** A thought of the author's own, written in English (the encoder, `all-MiniLM-L6-v2`,
  is English-trained, and so is the corpus, Elwes's translation), entered as a note. Expected = the
  propositions where the *Ethics* states the concept the thought is about, found by text search over
  the corpus, proposed by the executing agent with each statement beside it, and kept by the author
  before the commit.

## Rules

Frozen at the commit that adds the tasks.

1. **Clock.** It starts with X already selected in the assigned view (for T06–T10, once the note has
   been entered) and stops when the run is completed or at the cap of 3 minutes.
2. **Reached.** A note is reached when it is pointed at or selected (map), or opened (list), with its
   id and text on screen. Its proposition is read from its id (`PART{k}_…_P{nn}_…`), not from the author's reading
   of the text. Notes of the anchor's own proposition never count (T01–T05).
3. **Completed.** Notes from at least 2 distinct expected propositions are reached. A run not
   completed counts as the cap in time.
4. **Two runs per task,** one per view, the second on a later day. Switching views inside a run voids
   it as a measurement; it is logged, not repeated.
5. **Void.** A client defect that stops a view from working on a task voids the run and the whole
   task: both runs are left out, the defect is logged and the number of voids is reported.
6. **State.** The five notes of T06–T10 are entered before the first run of any task and nothing is
   entered afterwards, so both views of every task see the same state. If they cannot be entered by
   the end of day 6, T06–T10 are void.
7. **Order.** T01 to T10 within each pass, no repeats. The expected propositions are not edited after
   the commit.
8. **Decision.** Per task the two runs are compared: a completed run beats one not completed; if both
   are completed, or neither, the lower time wins when it is lower by at least 10 seconds, otherwise
   the task is a tie. The view that wins more tasks wins R5; on equal counts the list wins. Ties
   count for neither view. The list winning or tying is "R5 favours the list" in the day-7 table; the
   map winning is not.

## Limits (declared before any result)

- One user, who chose the anchors of T06–T10 and judges when a run is over.
- The tasks ask for related notes, which is what the list computes: in a perspective the vertical
  axis is already the real-distance order (`POC.md`, Scope changes, 2026-09-18), so on these tasks
  the map can at most match the list, and ties go to the list. R5 does not test what the map should
  add (an overview, what surrounds X). "R5 favours the list" is to be read as "for retrieving
  related notes the list suffices", not as "the map has no value".
- Reading a note costs one pointing action on the map and none on the list, which shows fifteen
  notes with their text at once. That cost is part of what R5 compares, not a defect of the
  comparison.
- T01–T05 measure whether a view recovers the *Ethics*' own explicit dependencies, which is narrower
  than "related".
- The expected sets of T01–T05 hold 30 to 50 of the 2,220 other notes (1.4% to 2.3%), and the list
  shows 15. If both views fail most tasks, most are ties, and ties go to the list.
- The second run of a task has seen what the first view shows for X. Alternating the first view and
  moving the second run to a later day reduce this; they do not remove it. The effect falls on
  whichever view is second, five tasks each.
- Times are taken by hand, hence the 10-second margin.
- T06–T10 differ in kind: their anchors are entered at runtime and are not part of the loaded
  corpus. Their expected sets are the author's ideas read against propositions proposed by the
  executing agent and kept by the author; T10's is the largest (six).
- The expected sets overlap: T02 and T06 share 2:7, T05 and T06 share 2:13, T03 and T10 share 3:11,
  T04 and T10 share 4:20, T07 and T09 share 2:26 and 2:39. The tasks are not independent.
- The five entered notes are part of the state, so they can occupy slots among the 15 nearest notes
  of any anchor. Both views see the same state, so this favours neither.
- Ten tasks: no inferential statistics; the outcome is a count, and it can rest on one task of
  difference.

## Prediction (registered 2026-09-21, before any run)

Not part of the decision rule; reported next to the result. In each view, a smaller proportion of
T06–T10 than of T01–T05 is completed (rule 3), counting non-void tasks. Why: the five entered notes
have no ε-edge (no other note within L2 ≤ 0.8), while 64% of the corpus notes have at least one; the
likely reason is that an English thought sits far from Elwes's sentences in the encoder's space. That
distance was not measured, only the absence of edges. The prediction fails in a view if T06–T10
completes the same proportion or more; it holds if it holds in both views. A group with no non-void
task is not evaluated. Rule 8 does not change.
